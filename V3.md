# V3 — Plasticity as a fluctuation

Status: thesis. Nothing built. Runnable in v1 today; does not depend on v2.

---

## 0. The move

V1 added mechanisms. V2 changes the substrate. Neither is novel and I have said
so plainly in the record.

V3 does not add a mechanism. It **deletes** most of the constants and derives
them from one measured quantity.

> **Learning rate is not a parameter. It is the local variance of the thing
> being learned.**

That is the whole claim. Everything below is consequence, experiment, or
falsifier.

**Superseded by §9.** The claim above survives, but its headline framing
("parsimony: six knobs, one quantity") does not — that framing is a physics
aesthetic and it collides with Adam. §9 replaces it with a stronger and more
falsifiable one: *two* timescales of variance, whose difference separates noise
from change. Read §9 before §2.

The precedent is physical, not neural. The fluctuation–dissipation theorem says
a system's response to a small applied force is fixed by its spontaneous
fluctuations at rest: χ = σ²/kT. You do not tune the response. You measure the
jitter and the response follows. Einstein's 1905 Brownian paper is the same
trick used the other way round — the noise was not a nuisance to be suppressed,
it was the measurement.

Our system currently suppresses and tunes its noise (`NOISE=0.35`). V3 reads it.

## 1. Why this system, specifically

Three of our own numbers say fixed constants are the disease:

- **HISTORY.md records five timescale-mismatch failures and five mechanisms that
  validated honestly and then turned harmful when the regime moved.** Both are
  one illness: a constant tuned in regime A, carried into regime B. More tuning
  cannot cure it. Derivation can.
- **`volatile-4` = 0.420, our worst task by a distance.** A fixed learning rate
  is *provably* wrong there — after a switch you want high plasticity, during a
  stable block you want low. There is no single correct value, which is exactly
  why tuning failed.
- **Capacity collapse 0.946 → 0.385 as combinations went 4 → 16.** New learning
  overwrites old because every synapse is equally plastic forever. A synapse
  that has settled should stop moving. Nothing in v1 lets it.

These are not three problems. They are one problem seen three times.

## 2. The equivalences

Each row is currently a separate mechanism carrying its own constant. Under V3
each is a reading of the same variable.

| v1 mechanism | v1 constant | v3 |
|---|---|---|
| learning rate | `LR=0.05` | plasticity ∝ local eligibility variance |
| exploration noise | `NOISE=0.35` | the action *is* a sample; exploration width = posterior width |
| replay priority | `HIPPO_WINDOW=20` (recency proxy) | replay what still fluctuates |
| sleep / consolidation | `SLEEP_REPLAY` schedule | consolidate when fluctuation collapses |
| homeostasis | `ETA_TH`, target rate | variance target replaces rate target |
| uncertainty signal (planned, step 8) | — | already present; nothing to add |

Six knobs, one measured quantity. **The success metric for V3 is the count of
hyperparameters deleted without performance loss.** That is an unusual metric
and a hard one to fake, which is the point.

## 3. Mechanism

Local, online, one extra state per synapse (rank-1 approximable — see PRD Q5,
which already costs 3× memory for eligibility and can share the factorisation).

Per synapse, track the running mean and variance of its own eligibility:

```
m_ij ← (1-α)·m_ij + α·e_ij
s_ij ← (1-α)·s_ij + α·(e_ij - m_ij)²
```

Plasticity is gated by it:

```
Δw_ij = η₀ · (s_ij / (s_ij + s₀)) · e_ij · NM
```

A synapse whose credit assignment has settled (low `s`) becomes rigid. One whose
credit is still ambiguous (high `s`) stays plastic. No schedule, no per-task
tuning, no separate consolidation pass.

**Action selection** samples from the vote distribution rather than adding fixed
noise. Wide votes → exploration; collapsed votes → commitment. This is Thompson
sampling, but arrived at from the substrate rather than imposed on it.

**Replay priority** becomes fluctuation, not TD error. This is a sharper
difference than it looks. TD-error priority replays an inherently stochastic
state *forever*, because it stays surprising. Variance priority stops replaying
it once the variance is aleatoric rather than epistemic — once you have learned
that it is genuinely random. `volatile-4` is precisely a task built out of
states like that.

## 4. Predictions, and what kills the thesis

1. **LR is deletable.** Variance-gated brainsim ≥ tuned-LR brainsim on `nway-8`
   and `lock-10` *with no per-task LR*. If it needs one, the thesis is dead.
2. **`volatile-4` moves the most.** The theory was proposed because of
   non-stationarity, so the biggest gain must appear there. If `volatile-4` does
   not improve, the thesis is wrong for the exact reason it was advanced.
3. **Automatic annealing.** Effective LR must fall over training with no
   schedule. Directly measurable; no excuse for not checking.
4. **Uncertainty is calibrated.** Variance on held-out compositional
   combinations must exceed variance on seen ones. Our held-out score is 0.002
   — confidently wrong. If variance does not flag those cases, "variance =
   epistemic uncertainty" is false and everything above collapses with it.
5. **Variance-priority replay beats TD-error-priority replay on `volatile-4`,
   and only there.** A win everywhere would mean I measured something else.

### The kill condition

The variance estimator has a window, α. **If performance is as sensitive to α as
it was to `LR`, we have renamed a hyperparameter, not removed one.** This must be
tested first and reported whether or not it is convenient. The defence, if it is
needed, is a spectrum of estimators at multiple α (synaptic cascade models,
Fusi & Drew 2005) rather than one — but a defence invented after a failed test
is a worse thing than the failure.

## 5. Prior art, stated honestly

Everything here has a relative. The parts are not the contribution.

- **Neural sampling** — Hoyer & Hyvärinen 2003; Buesing et al. 2011; Berkes et
  al. 2011 (spontaneous cortical activity matches the prior). *The
  representation claim is theirs.*
- **Synaptic plasticity as Bayesian inference** — Aitchison et al. 2021, Nature
  Neuroscience: synaptic learning rate ∝ posterior uncertainty. **This is the
  closest existing work and it has the core idea at the synapse level.**
- **Uncertainty-modulated learning rate** — Behrens 2007; Yu & Dayan; Kalman TD
  (Kakade & Dayan). *Theirs.*
- **Uncertainty-driven exploration** — Thompson sampling; Bootstrapped DQN
  (Osband 2016); deep ensembles (Lakshminarayanan 2017). *Theirs.*
- **SGD noise as temperature** — Chaudhari & Soatto. *Theirs.*

What is left: Aitchison's is a synapse-level theory fitted to plasticity data.
Osband's is exploration only. Berkes' is perception only. Behrens' is a fitted
Bayesian observer, not a mechanism. **Nobody has run the deletion experiment** —
one measured fluctuation replacing the learning rate *and* the exploration
temperature *and* the replay priority *and* the consolidation schedule
*simultaneously*, in a running agent, with an ablation record.

The novelty is unification and deletion, not a new mechanism. That is the right
shape to be aiming at. Einstein added no forces; he removed the ether and
absolute time.

## 6. Relationship to v2 and to steps 7–13

V3 is **orthogonal to the substrate rewrite**. Variance-gated plasticity is a
change to the update rule and can be tested in v1 this week. This is good news:
it decouples the novelty question from the continuous-time port, which was
never where novelty was going to come from.

It also **absorbs step 8**. Step 8 was "a local signal for whether a decision
was worth learning from," which I assessed as a rediscovery of advantage. Under
V3 that signal is not added — it is already the quantity driving everything else.

**Step 7a (external baselines) remains a hard prerequisite.** Every prediction
above is a comparison. Without tabular Q-learning on `lock`, logistic regression
on `nway`/`xor`, and a memory baseline on `tmaze`, "≥ tuned-LR brainsim" is a

Smallest decisive test, in order:

## 8. Is this moving away from the brain?

Partly toward, partly away. Both directions are worth stating precisely, because
the "away" one is a real cost and not a quibble.

### Toward — the plasticity rule itself is textbook neuroscience

- **BCM (Bienenstock, Cooper & Munro 1982)** already gates plasticity on a
  second moment. The sliding modification threshold is θ_M ∝ ⟨y²⟩ — the
  time-averaged *squared* postsynaptic activity. A variance-like quantity
  controlling plasticity is not an import from physics; it is the oldest
  well-supported metaplasticity model there is.
- **Metaplasticity** generally — Abraham & Bear 1996, "plasticity of synaptic
  plasticity" — is the observation that prior activity changes how plastic a
  synapse is next.
- **Cascade synapses (Fusi, Drew & Abbott 2005)**: internal states make a
  synapse progressively harder to modify. "Settled → rigid" is their mechanism.
- **Aitchison et al. 2021** derives synaptic learning rate ∝ posterior
  uncertainty and fits it to plasticity data.
- **Prediction 3 has already been observed in vivo.** Churchland et al. 2010,
  *Stimulus onset quenches neural variability*, across many cortical areas — and
  variability falls with training. The automatic annealing V3 predicts is a
  measured cortical phenomenon.
- **Spine volumes fluctuate intrinsically**, largely independent of activity
  (Loewenstein, Kuras & Rumpel 2011). Per-synapse fluctuation is a real,
  measured quantity, not a modelling convenience.

### Toward — and we already half-built it

`ADAPTIVE=True` in v1 is described in the source as *"NE/ACh: volatility from
reward-rate change → LR, noise"*. That is V3 in miniature: a measured
fluctuation modulating learning rate and exploration. It validated. **V3 is
ADAPTIVE made local and given six jobs instead of two**, which means the class
of mechanism has already survived our own ablation record rather than arriving
untested.

There is a second, sharper argument sitting in our defaults. `ETA_TH=0.0`:
first-moment homeostasis was measured useless because **k-WTA already pins the
mean firing rate to exactly k/n**. If the first moment is clamped by the
architecture, it carries no information. The variance is the only free statistic
left to read. Our own ablation shut the first-moment door.

### Away — the parsimony is more physicist than neuroscientist

This is the honest cost. **The brain does not unify its uncertainty signals into
one scalar.** Yu & Dayan — which v1 already cites — explicitly *separates*
expected uncertainty (ACh) from unexpected uncertainty (NE) into two
neuromodulators with different roles. §2's "six knobs, one quantity" is a
parsimony aesthetic imported from physics. It is more unified than the organ.

Two smaller departures:

- **Per-synapse vs per-neuron.** BCM's threshold is postsynaptic and
  per-*neuron*; that is what the data support. §3 specifies a per-*synapse*
  variance of eligibility, which is a stronger and less evidenced claim about
  what a synapse can locally access.
- **Sampling is a live debate, not a fact.** "The action is a sample from the
  posterior" (Buesing, Berkes) competes with probabilistic population codes (Ma,
  Beck, Latham & Pouget), where uncertainty lives in the width and gain of a
  population rather than in temporal sampling. V3 picks a side.

### Reconciliation — declared in advance, not as a rescue

The brain-plausible form of V3 is a **small spectrum of estimators at different
α**, not one: fast and slow second moments, mapping onto the expected/unexpected
uncertainty split. That is exactly what §4's kill condition offers as a defence,
which is the danger — a defence invented after a failed test is worse than the
failure.

So it is committed to now, before anything runs:

> **The theory is "plasticity is gated by a measured local second moment."** The
> number of estimators (one, or a small fixed spectrum) is a design choice
> *within* the theory, and both variants are declared before the experiment. If
> a single estimator fails and a two-estimator version works, that is a result,
> not a rescue. If *neither* works, or if either needs per-task tuning of α, the
> theory is dead as stated in §4.

### Consequent change to §7

**Do the per-neuron (BCM-style) version first.** It is better evidenced, cheaper,
and needs no rank-1 factorisation. Per-synapse variance becomes the second
experiment, run only if the per-neuron one earns it.

## 9. The reframe: not brain-like, but brain-sourced

The constraint changes from *"is this how a brain works"* to:

> Take what works in a brain **that is not in LLMs or neural nets**, and judge it
> by whether it pushes things forward generally.

This is a better constraint and it changes the thesis. Two consequences.

### The §8 cost disappears

§8's main objection was that "six knobs, one quantity" is more unified than the
organ — the brain deliberately splits uncertainty across neuromodulators (ACh
expected, NE unexpected; Yu & Dayan). Under the old frame that was a departure
to be justified. Under the new frame it is not a cost at all; it is a free
design choice. Nothing there needs defending.

### But the parsimony framing dies anyway — because that headline is Adam

Confront this directly, because it is the strongest objection to V3 and it is
not a brain objection at all:

**Adam already gates the learning rate on a local second moment.** Its
denominator is √⟨g²⟩ — a per-parameter running second moment of the gradient.
Every neural net trained in the last decade does per-parameter
variance-modulated plasticity. "Learning rate from local variance" is not the
thing brains have and nets lack. It is in the default optimiser.

Except **the sign is opposite.**

| | statistic | high variance ⇒ |
|---|---|---|
| Adam | one second moment of the gradient | **smaller** step |
| V3 §3 | one second moment of eligibility | **larger** step |

Both are right, about different things. Adam reads variance as *measurement
noise* — aleatoric — so shrinking is correct. V3 reads it as *ignorance* —
epistemic — so growing is correct. A single estimator cannot tell them apart,
which means **Adam conflates them, and so did §3.**

### The actual claim

> **Adam has one second moment and always shrinks. Brains have at least two, at
> different timescales, and their difference decides whether to shrink or grow.**

Slow estimator ≈ expected uncertainty ≈ how noisy this has *always* been ≈ ACh.
Fast estimator ≈ current variability ≈ NE. Their relative excess is the gate:

```
gate = max(0, s_fast - s_slow) / (s_fast + s_slow + eps)
```

- stable and noisy → fast ≈ slow → gate ≈ 0. Do not chase the noise. *(Adam)*
- settled and quiet → both small → gate ≈ 0. Do not overwrite what is known.
- something just changed → fast ≫ slow → gate → 1. Become plastic now.

This is a **Kalman gain, P/(P+R), approximated by two EMAs per parameter and no
covariance matrix.** The Bayesian filtering world has had the process-noise vs
observation-noise split forever (Kalman-TD; Kakade & Dayan), and it is precisely
the aleatoric/epistemic distinction. What it has not had is a version cheap
enough to be a drop-in optimiser: two extra floats per parameter, no matrix.

Note it also **deletes `s₀`** from §3, which was a hand-set constant standing in
for observation noise. The slow estimator measures it instead.

### Why this explains our own worst number

`volatile-4` = 0.420. A volatile task is built from states that are *stochastic
but stable* sitting next to states that *just changed*. To a single-timescale
estimator these are identical — both look like high variance. That is not an
incidental weakness of v1; it is the exact failure this gate is shaped to fix,
and it is why prediction 2 (§4) is the one that matters.

### Why it passes the "pushes things forward" test

The claim is now legible to someone who does not care about brains:

> Optimisers have no non-stationarity story. Adam cannot distinguish a noisy
> gradient from a changed objective, so continual and online learning fall back
> on replay, resets, or regularisation. A per-parameter signal that separates
> the two would be a drop-in modification to Adam.

That is testable **outside brainsim entirely** — a small non-stationary
supervised benchmark, Adam vs two-timescale Adam, no spiking, no eligibility, no
replay. Which happens to solve the problem that has dogged this project since
step 7a: it is an external baseline that exists whether or not our agent is any
good.

### What brains have that nets lack — the general list

For the record, since the reframe invites the question. Ranked by how absent
they are from current practice:

1. **No train/test boundary.** Continuous learning during deployment, without
   catastrophic forgetting. Nothing in an LLM does this. Everything below serves
   it.
2. **Multiple plasticity timescales that decide what to overwrite.** ← V3.
3. **Offline consolidation.** Sleep, replay, compression into slow weights. No
   deployed analogue.
4. **Neuromodulation** — a global scalar broadcast that retunes the whole
   network's learning in one step. No analogue at all.
5. **Local credit assignment.** Here nets arguably *win*; this is a constraint
   we have accepted, not an advantage we have shown.

V1 and v2 sit mostly in 5, which is why the novelty answer kept coming back
thin. V3 sits in 2, which serves 1.

### Consequent changes

- **§4 prediction 1 is downgraded.** "LR is deletable" is no longer the headline
  test; deleting a knob is a parsimony win, not a capability win. The headline
  test is now prediction 2, `volatile-4`.
- **§4's kill condition is retired as stated.** The two-estimator version is no
  longer a fallback to be declared in advance — it *is* the theory. The
  single-estimator version is now the ablation, and it is predicted to fail on
  `volatile-4` specifically.
- **New first experiment, ahead of §7.** Non-stationary supervised toy, Adam vs
  two-timescale gate. No brainsim involved. Days, not weeks, and it is the
  external evidence this project has never had.
